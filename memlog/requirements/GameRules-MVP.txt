RA11y — MVP Game Rules
Per-Screen Strings, Accessibility Labels, and Pedagogical Design

Date: 2026-02-20 (revised)
Scope: iOS MVP (VoiceOver required ON; no preview mode)
Style: High-contrast, Dynamic Type friendly; no meaning conveyed by color alone.

===========================================================================
CONVENTIONS
===========================================================================

Localization
------------
- All user-visible strings must be localized (String Catalog: Localizable.xcstrings).
- This document provides: localization keys and default English values.

Accessibility Standards
-----------------------
- Every interactive element: accessibilityLabel (required), accessibilityHint (if it adds value).
- Every non-interactive image: accessibilityHidden = true.
- Reading order must be deterministic and linearizable.
- No accessibility meaning conveyed by color alone.

D&D Tone Guide
--------------
- Narration is authoritative, brief, atmospheric. The hub uses a neutral host; Enchanter/Rogue may use bardic/DM flavor; Crystal Resonance uses the **Resonance Guide** (`dm.label` for that flow).
- Player is always addressed as "Adventurer" or "you" (second person).
- Mistakes are not failures, they are "missteps" or "fumbles."
- The DM never mocks. Failure is narrated with consequence, not shame.
- Success language is earned: "Well done." / "Trial passed." / "The vault yields."

===========================================================================
PEDAGOGY FRAMEWORK — Three VoiceOver Paradigm Shifts
===========================================================================

RA11y teaches VoiceOver through three progressive games, each targeting one
core paradigm shift that trips up new VoiceOver users.

Game 1 — The Enchanter's Trial (Find & Focus)
----------------------------------------------
Paradigm shift: VoiceOver reads ONE element at a time.
Users expect to see the whole screen at once. VoiceOver changes the model:
swiping right moves focus to the next element. The screen is now linear.
Teaching goal: Swipe to move focus. Listen to identify. Double-tap to act.

Game 2 — The Rogue's Gauntlet (Activate / Double-Tap)
------------------------------------------------------
Paradigm shift: Tapping no longer activates.
With VoiceOver on, a single tap on an element moves focus to it — it does
NOT activate it. Double-tap (anywhere on screen) activates the focused element.
This is the single most common source of confusion for new VoiceOver users.
Teaching goal: Touch = examine (focus). Double-tap = invoke (activate).

Game 3 — Crystal Resonance (Scroll Hunt)
------------------------------------------
Paradigm shift: Single-finger swipe no longer scrolls.
VoiceOver "steals" the single-finger swipe for focus navigation. To scroll
a page, the user must use three fingers. Users get stuck at the bottom of
visible content with no knowledge of how to go deeper.
Teaching goal: Three-finger swipe scrolls the page. Swipe to navigate, three-finger to descend the crystal shaft.

Level Progression (Shared Across All Games)
--------------------------------------------
Each game follows a 4-step arc:

  L0 — Prologue (No timer)
    The DM introduces the quest and the VoiceOver skill.
    The player is TOLD what is different about VoiceOver before they try.
    No challenge yet — just orientation and curiosity.

  L1 — First Attempt (No timer)
    Minimal complexity. One target. One or two decoys maximum.
    The player performs the skill exactly once with no time pressure.
    Mistakes allowed. Restart available. The goal is confidence.

  L2 — Rising Challenge (Soft timer — optional, generous)
    More elements. Similar-sounding or closely placed targets.
    A soft timer introduces gentle urgency but with enough time to succeed.
    Mistakes begin to count. The player is being assessed.

  L3 — Timed Trial (Hard timer + progress bar)
    Full complexity. Hard time limit. Failure occurs when time runs out.
    The progress bar depletes visually. VoiceOver announces at thresholds.
    This is the scored challenge. Rank is awarded here.

===========================================================================
TIMER UI — Shared (L2 Optional, L3 Required)
===========================================================================

Visual Design
-------------
- A horizontal progress bar that depletes left-to-right as time runs out.
- Bar width shrinks as time passes — NOT purely color-coded (accessible).
- At ≥50%: full-height bar.
- At 25–49%: bar height reduces to 75% (subtle urgency cue).
- At <25%: bar height reduces to 50%, animated pulse.
- Text label below bar: "{N}s remaining" — NOT updated every second.
- Text updates: every 10s, then every 5s in the final 15s, then every second
  in the final 5s.

This prevents the visual from flickering and VoiceOver from being overwhelmed.

Localization Keys (Visual)
--------------------------
KEY: hud.timer.label            VALUE: Time Remaining
KEY: hud.timer.format           VALUE: {seconds}s remaining

VoiceOver Behavior
------------------
The timer group is a SINGLE accessibility element. It does NOT update
continuously — doing so would interrupt the player's focus navigation.

Update thresholds for VoiceOver announcement:
  - At 75% elapsed    → KEY: a11y.timer.75pct   VALUE: Three-quarters of your time is gone.
  - At 50% elapsed    → KEY: a11y.timer.50pct   VALUE: Half your time remains.
  - At 25% elapsed    → KEY: a11y.timer.25pct   VALUE: A quarter of your time remains.
  - At 10s remaining  → KEY: a11y.timer.10s     VALUE: Ten seconds.
  - At 5s remaining   → announce every second:
      KEY: a11y.timer.5s VALUE: Five.
      KEY: a11y.timer.4s VALUE: Four.
      KEY: a11y.timer.3s VALUE: Three.
      KEY: a11y.timer.2s VALUE: Two.
      KEY: a11y.timer.1s VALUE: One.

Timer Group Accessibility Element
-----------------------------------
accessibilityLabel:
  KEY: a11y.timer.group
  VALUE: Time remaining. {seconds} seconds.

accessibilityHint:
  KEY: a11y.timer.group.hint
  VALUE: Complete your objective before time runs out.

Timeout Announcement (generic fallback)
----------------------------------------
KEY: a11y.timer.timeout VALUE: Time's up.

Game-specific timeout announcements override this — see each game below.

GAME 1: THE ENCHANTER'S TRIAL
======================================================================

Game ID: simon_find_focus
VoiceOver Skill: Focus navigation (swipe left/right) + targeted activation (double-tap)

Narrative Context
-----------------
In the Enchanter's tower, relics are arranged on a shelf. The Enchanter
calls the name of one relic. The apprentice must navigate their awareness
(VoiceOver focus) through the shelf — one relic at a time — until the
named relic is reached, then invoke it (double-tap).

Why this teaches the skill:
  VoiceOver makes the screen LINEAR. You cannot see everything at once —
  you hear one element at a time as you move focus. Swiping is the only
  way to explore. This game makes that constraint the entire mechanic.

GAME 2: THE ROGUE'S GAUNTLET
======================================================================

Game ID: bomb_defusal_wires
VoiceOver Skill: Double-tap activation — understanding that touch ≠ action

Narrative Context
-----------------
A rogue's greatest tool is precision: touch to sense, act to strike.
In the Trap Room, magical seals line a locked door. Each seal is labeled.
One seal must be severed. The wrong seal springs the trap.

With VoiceOver, touching a seal reads it aloud — it does NOT activate it.
Only a deliberate double-tap invokes the seal. This is the Rogue's advantage:
examine first, then strike with intention.

Why this teaches the skill:
  This is the most common VoiceOver confusion: "I tapped it but nothing happened."
  With VoiceOver, tapping once MOVES FOCUS (examines). Double-tapping ACTIVATES.
  The trap theme makes "accidental activation" feel consequential and teaches
  the player to be deliberate. Touch = sense. Double-tap = sever.

The relic sings from deep in the crystal shaft—taller than any one screen.

along the shaft unfold one ring at a time.

Why this teaches the skill:
  With VoiceOver active, swiping with one finger navigates between elements.
  Scrolling the page requires THREE fingers. New users get stranded at the
  last visible item with no idea how to go deeper. Crystal Resonance makes
  "three-finger scroll" the only path forward — teaching the gesture by
  making it necessary.

Note on detection:
  We do not detect the exact scroll gesture. We observe the scroll outcome
  (did content scroll?). Validate via scroll progress, then activation of
  the target. See GameSpec-ScrollHunt.txt.
