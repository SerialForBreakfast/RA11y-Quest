RA11y — The Arcanist's Tower
Rotor Quest Game Design Document

Date: 2026-04-22
Status: Refined Draft — actionable design plan
Source: ArcanistsTower GDD.docx v1.0 draft, expanded into implementation tasks

===============================================================================
1. OVERVIEW
===============================================================================

The Arcanist's Tower is RA11y's rotor quest. Where Enchanter's Trial teaches
linear VoiceOver navigation and double-tap activation, Crystal Resonance teaches
three-finger scroll, and Banishment teaches two-finger scrub escape, Arcanist's
Tower teaches the VoiceOver rotor: the gesture that changes what swipe gestures
search for or adjust.

Fantasy framing:

  The rotor is the spell wheel. Each rotor setting is a magical discipline.
  Choosing the wrong setting is casting the wrong school of magic for the room.

Core lesson:

  The rotor is not just a menu. It changes the meaning of your next swipe.

Player should leave knowing:

  1. How to invoke the rotor with a two-finger twist.
  2. How to select a rotor setting.
  3. Why Headings, Links, and Adjustable are useful in different contexts.
  4. How to recognize when the room demands a specific rotor setting.

Non-negotiable accessibility goal:

  Every challenge must genuinely require the intended rotor setting. The quest
  fails pedagogically if brute-force one-element swiping is equally viable.

===============================================================================
2. CURRICULUM FIT
===============================================================================

Skill taught:

  VoiceOver rotor: two-finger twist to choose a navigation or adjustment mode,
  then swipe according to the selected rotor setting.

One-sentence lesson:

  When a screen has a kind of thing you need, use the rotor to make swipe gestures
  jump directly to that kind of thing.

Prerequisites:

  - Enchanter's Trial: swipe through elements and double-tap to activate.
  - Crystal Resonance: understand that multi-finger VoiceOver gestures can control
    the focused accessibility surface.
  - Banishment: two-finger gesture vocabulary and confidence.

Downstream skills:

  - Efficient web/document navigation.
  - Form adjustment and picker interaction.
  - Advanced rotor settings such as Characters, Words, Containers, Landmarks, and
    custom rotors.

Paradigm shift:

  Player moves from linear exploration to filtered exploration.

===============================================================================
3. QUEST STRUCTURE
===============================================================================

The quest should follow RA11y's three-stage design rule while preserving the
original floor fantasy.

Stage 1 — Practice: The Arcanist Speaks + Guided Floors
-------------------------------------------------------

Purpose:

  Teach the rotor gesture and the three implemented disciplines without ranking.

Content:

  - L0 Prologue: spell wheel lesson.
  - L1a Codex: Headings with 3 headings, untimed.
  - L2a Tome: Links with 3 links, untimed.
  - L3a Archery: Adjustable with 1 arrow, untimed.

Exit condition:

  Player completes one successful use of each implemented rotor mode.

Stage 2 — Trial: The Shifting Tower
-----------------------------------

Purpose:

  Test recognition under time pressure.

Content:

  - Random sequence of Headings, Links, and Adjustable rooms.
  - Timer hard.
  - No explicit "choose Headings now" instruction.
  - Environmental and VoiceOver cues reveal which mode is appropriate.

Exit condition:

  Complete all rounds before timer expires.

Stage 3 — Lights Off: The Blind Observatory
-------------------------------------------

Purpose:

  Prove the rotor skill with visual cues minimized.

Content:

  - Same round types as Stage 2.
  - Darkened screen.
  - VoiceOver, haptics, and short audio cues carry floor identity.
  - No visual labels beyond minimal status/anchor.

Exit condition:

  Complete all rounds before timer expires.

Why this mapping:

  The original L1/L2/L4/Boss floor structure is strong, but RA11y's current design
  process wants three stages. This plan keeps the floor fantasy while making the
  implementation path clear.

===============================================================================
4. ROTOR DISCIPLINES
===============================================================================

Discipline A — Headings: The Codex Floor
----------------------------------------

Rotor setting:

  Headings.

Real-world mapping:

  Jumping between headings in documents, articles, settings pages, and web pages.

Challenge fantasy:

  The Arcanist gives a riddle. The answer is hidden under a chapter heading.

Core mechanic:

  The screen contains dense lore text broken into headings. The player sets the
  rotor to Headings and swipes between headings to find and activate the named
  chapter.

Why the rotor is required:

  Without Headings, the player must swipe through body paragraphs and decorative
  text. With Headings, the player jumps directly between chapters.

Practice version:

  - 3 headings.
  - Target name is literal.
  - No timer.
  - Example target: "Chapter of Ash."

Trial version:

  - 8 headings.
  - Similar labels included.
  - Timer active.
  - Wrong heading costs time or mistake.

Lights Off version:

  - Same accessibility tree.
  - Visual text dimmed.
  - VoiceOver heading labels carry all information.

Candidate heading pool:

  - Chapter of Ash
  - Chapter of Cinders
  - The Iron Compact
  - The Iron Accord
  - Verse of Flame
  - Verse of Ember
  - The Sapphire Oath
  - The Sapphire Path
  - Index of Moons
  - Index of Stars
  - The Silent Gate
  - The Sealed Gate

Success:

  "Correct chapter. The Codex opens."

Wrong action:

  "That is not the chapter. The tower steals time."

Implementation requirements:

  - Each chapter title is a real accessibility heading.
  - Body text remains in the accessibility tree so the rotor has obvious value.
  - Target heading is a Button or has explicit activation action.
  - Programmatic focus should land near the instruction/objective, then allow
    normal rotor use.

Acceptance criteria:

  - Device VoiceOver: Headings rotor jumps only chapter titles.
  - Wrong heading activation is announced and tracked.
  - Timer does not speak continuously.
  - Brute-force swiping is intentionally slower than rotor use.

Discipline B — Links: The Tome Floor
------------------------------------

Rotor setting:

  Links.

Real-world mapping:

  Jumping between links in web pages, rich text, and documents.

Challenge fantasy:

  A cursed tome contains several rune-links. One breaks the seal; the rest are
  traps.

Core mechanic:

  The screen contains dense lore prose with embedded link elements. The player
  sets the rotor to Links, jumps rune-to-rune, and activates the named rune.

Why the rotor is required:

  Without Links, the user must traverse all surrounding paragraphs. With Links,
  only runes are enumerated.

Practice version:

  - 3 rune links.
  - Distinct names.
  - No traps.
  - No timer.

Trial version:

  - 6 rune links.
  - Near-similar labels.
  - Wrong rune triggers time penalty/mistake.

Lights Off version:

  - Visual rune glow mostly hidden.
  - Link labels and hints carry all semantics.

Candidate rune pool:

  - Seal of Binding
  - Seal of Bonding
  - Ember Rune
  - Ember Mark
  - Shadow Glyph
  - Shadow Sign
  - Rune of Passage
  - Rune of Passing
  - Mirror Sigil
  - Mirror Seal
  - Ward of Dawn
  - Ward of Dusk

Success:

  "Correct rune. The tome releases its seal."

Wrong action:

  "A trap springs. Shadows close in."

Implementation requirements:

  - Rune text must be real Link accessibility elements.
  - Hints: "Double-tap to invoke this rune."
  - Surrounding prose remains accessible.
  - Wrong link and success paths update state immediately.

Acceptance criteria:

  - Device VoiceOver: Links rotor jumps only rune links.
  - Player can complete without visually reading the page.
  - Wrong rune is clearly distinguished from correct rune.

Discipline C — Adjustable: The Archery Floor
--------------------------------------------

Rotor setting:

  Adjustable.

Real-world mapping:

  Adjusting sliders, steppers, pickers, ratings, values, and custom adjustable
  controls.

Challenge fantasy:

  A magical bow must be drawn to the exact weight needed to strike a distant lock.

Core mechanic:

  The player focuses a custom adjustable draw-weight element. With the Adjustable
  rotor, swipe up increases draw weight and swipe down decreases it. Double-tap
  fires the arrow.

Why the rotor is required:

  The draw weight element exposes adjustable behavior. Vertical swipes change the
  value only when VoiceOver is using the appropriate adjustment model.

Practice version:

  - 1 arrow.
  - Required value: 5 of 10.
  - Tolerance: ±2.
  - No timer.

Trial version:

  - 3 arrows.
  - Required values: 5 of 10, 7 of 10, 8 of 12.
  - Tolerance narrows from ±1 to exact.
  - Timer active.

Lights Off version:

  - Bow visuals suppressed.
  - Current value and required value are VoiceOver-first.
  - Haptics indicate under/near/exact.

Success:

  "The arrow strikes true. One seal breaks."

Undershoot:

  "The arrow falls short. Draw more."

Overshoot:

  "Too much force. Ease the draw."

Implementation requirements:

  - Draw weight element has `.accessibilityAdjustableAction`.
  - Label: "Draw weight."
  - Value: "[n] of [max]."
  - Hint: "Swipe up or down to adjust. Double-tap to fire."
  - The fire action should be distinct from adjustment.

Acceptance criteria:

  - Device VoiceOver announces value changes natively.
  - Swipe up/down changes only the draw weight while focused.
  - Exact hit, undershoot, and overshoot are testable.

===============================================================================
5. BOSS / TRIAL STRUCTURE: THE SHIFTING TOWER
===============================================================================

The original Boss concept becomes Stage 2 and Stage 3's main structure.

Round count:

  Recommended initial value: 5 rounds.

Why 5, not 7:

  Rotor selection itself is slow and cognitively heavy. Start with 5 to avoid
  fatigue. Increase only after device playtesting shows runs are too short.

Round pool:

  - Codex room: Headings.
  - Tome room: Links.
  - Archery room: Adjustable.

Round selection:

  - Randomized order per run.
  - Avoid same discipline more than twice in a row.
  - Ensure every run includes at least one instance of each discipline.

No explicit setting instruction:

  Stage 2 and 3 should not say "Use Headings" or "Use Links." They should present
  a room cue and let the player infer the correct rotor.

Cue model:

  Codex:
    Visual: scroll or codex pedestal.
    VoiceOver: "A chapter awaits you."
    Audio: page turn / parchment shift.

  Tome:
    Visual: open tome with glowing runes.
    VoiceOver: "A rune must be broken."
    Audio: low magical hum with small rune chime.

  Archery:
    Visual: bow and distant lock.
    VoiceOver: "The lock must be struck true."
    Audio: bowstring tension.

Completion:

  - Each successful round advances immediately.
  - After all rounds, tower opens and result is recorded.

Failure:

  - Timer expires: Defeated.
  - Wrong activation/shot/rune adds mistake and costs time.
  - Wrong rotor choice is not directly observable from app code, so design should
    not depend on detecting rotor selection itself. Instead, wrong attempts produce
    instructional feedback when an activation or fire happens incorrectly.

===============================================================================
6. SCORING
===============================================================================

Practice stage:

  - No rank.
  - Mistakes are teaching moments.

Trial stage:

  - Hard timer: 75 seconds for first tuning pass.
  - Base completion required.
  - Mistakes lower rank.
  - Remaining time breaks ties.

Lights Off stage:

  - Hard timer: 90 seconds for first tuning pass.
  - Same rank rules, slightly more forgiving due reduced visuals.

Rank draft:

  Legendary:
    Complete all rounds, 0 mistakes, with at least 25% time remaining.

  Skilled:
    Complete all rounds with 0-2 mistakes.

  Novice:
    Complete all rounds with 3+ mistakes.

  Defeated:
    Timer expires before all rounds complete.

Mistake rules:

  - Wrong heading: +1 mistake.
  - Wrong link: +1 mistake.
  - Wrong bow shot: +1 mistake.
  - Repeated wrong action during cooldown: ignored, not multiplied.

Time penalties:

  - Wrong heading/link: optional -5 seconds in Stage 2.
  - Wrong bow shot: consumes arrow attempt or adds +1 mistake, but avoid both
    consuming attempt and adding harsh time penalty until playtested.

Calibration notes:

  Initial timers are guesses. Device testing with VoiceOver must set final values.

===============================================================================
7. ACCESSIBILITY MODEL
===============================================================================

Global rules:

  - VoiceOver must be required for real quest entry.
  - Each discipline must use real platform semantics where possible.
  - Do not fake rotor behavior with custom buttons unless a platform API is
    unavailable.
  - No visual-only cues.
  - No continuous narration during active timed play.

Prologue:

  - One lesson card and Continue button.
  - Diagram is decorative with a descriptive accessibility label.
  - No timer.

Codex:

  - Chapter titles use Heading trait.
  - Target chapter is activatable.
  - Body text remains accessible.

Tome:

  - Runes use Link trait.
  - Rune labels must be distinct and localizable.
  - Body text remains accessible.

Archery:

  - Draw weight uses Adjustable trait.
  - Value changes via native VoiceOver adjustment announcement.

Timer:

  - Stage 2: announce 50%, 25%, 10 seconds, then 5-4-3-2-1.
  - Stage 3: announce 50%, 25%, 10 seconds, then 5-4-3-2-1.
  - Do not announce every second.

Focus:

  - On room transition, focus should move to the room objective/status region.
  - For Adjustable rooms, after objective is read, focus may move to the draw
    weight element.
  - Do not programmatically fight rotor navigation during active play.

Wrong-action announcements:

  - Immediate.
  - Short.
  - Rate-limited to avoid speech spam.

===============================================================================
8. UI / ART DIRECTION
===============================================================================

Overall:

  A vertical wizard tower with distinct floors. Each floor has a clear discipline
  identity but shares the RA11y dark fantasy visual language.

Prologue:

  - Arcanist portrait or silhouette.
  - Spell wheel diagram: two fingers rotating a dial.
  - Large lesson card.

Codex:

  - Long parchment/codex page.
  - Headings as large chapter plaques.
  - Body text visually dense but readable.

Tome:

  - Open cursed tome.
  - Glowing rune links embedded in prose.
  - Trap runes visually tempting but not overwhelming.

Archery:

  - Bow silhouette foreground.
  - Distant lock or target.
  - Draw meter as accessible adjustable control.

Shifting Tower:

  - Room cue changes per round.
  - Shared timer and progress indicator.
  - Avoid over-animating transitions; VoiceOver needs stable context.

Lights Off:

  - Dark veil.
  - Minimal discipline anchor remains visible.
  - Full semantics via VoiceOver/haptics/audio.

Asset candidates:

  - arcanist_tower_bg
  - arcanist_portrait
  - arcanist_spell_wheel_diagram
  - arcanist_codex_bg
  - arcanist_tome_bg
  - arcanist_archery_bg
  - arcanist_bow
  - arcanist_target_lock
  - arcanist_success_flare
  - arcanist_lights_off_anchor

Asset approach:

  Build greybox first. Use final art only after rotor semantics are verified on
  device. This follows the Banishment lesson from Crystal Resonance: beautiful art
  cannot come before the accessibility contract.

9. IMPLEMENTATION ARCHITECTURE
===============================================================================

Suggested files:

  RA11y-iOS/RA11y-iOS/Games/iOSArcanistsTowerView.swift
  RA11y-iOS/RA11y-iOS/Games/iOSArcanistsTowerComponents.swift
  RA11y-iOS/RA11y-iOS/Games/iOSArcanistsTowerArt.swift

Core types:

  enum ArcanistsTowerPhase
    - prologue
    - practiceCodex
    - practiceTome
    - practiceArchery
    - trial(roundIndex)
    - lightsOff(roundIndex)
    - completed

  enum TowerDiscipline
    - headings
    - links
    - adjustable

  enum TowerRoundState
    - intro
    - active
    - success
    - mistake
    - timeout

  struct TowerRound
    - id
    - discipline
    - targetID
    - targetLabel
    - decoys
    - timeBudget
    - lightsOff

ViewModel responsibilities:

  - Own phase, round list, current target, mistakes, timer, result.
  - Generate deterministic rounds for tests/screenshot scenes.
  - Handle heading activation.
  - Handle link activation.
  - Handle draw-weight adjustment and fire action.
  - Start/stop timers with VoiceOver grace period.
  - Record GameSession results.
  - Post announcements through one method.

View responsibilities:

  - Render the current floor.
  - Expose correct accessibility semantics.
  - Avoid computing game outcomes.
  - Keep decorative art hidden from accessibility.

Testing hooks:

  - Inject seeded round generator.
  - Inject clock or use deterministic time advancement in ViewModel tests.
  - Screenshot scene can force specific phase and round.

11. OPEN DECISIONS
===============================================================================

1. Quest naming:

  "The Arcanist's Tower" is grammatically clean for one mentor. "Arcanists'
  Tower" implies a guild tower. Pick one and use it consistently.

2. Stage count:

  The doc now maps floor fantasy into RA11y's 3-stage rule. Confirm whether this
  quest should be an exception or follow the standard.

3. Form Controls and Characters:

  Original draft reserved Form Controls and Characters. Keep them post-v1 unless
  a strong mechanic emerges.

4. Boss timer:

  60 seconds may be tight for real rotor use. Start at 75 seconds for Stage 2 and
  tune down only after testing.

5. Wrong rotor detection:

  Apps generally cannot reliably know the current rotor selection. Design should
  avoid scoring "wrong rotor selected" directly. Score wrong outcomes instead.

6. Adjustable activation:

  Need a clear way to fire after setting draw weight. A focused adjustable element
  can expose value changes, but firing may need a separate Button or custom action.
  Device testing should decide.
